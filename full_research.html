My Research

I'm fascinated by the interactions between light and matter! My work focuses on using many body quantum simulation techniques, primarily Real-Time Time-Dependent Density Functional Theory (Real-Time TDDFT), to explore and understand plasmonic phenomena.

When certain materials contain free electrons, these electrons can collectively oscillate at specific frequencies, forming waves across the electron "sea". When such collective oscillations are confined to the material's surface, they're known as Localized Surface Plasmons (LSPs). Why is this exciting? Because LSPs allow us to control and manipulate electromagnetic radiation beyond the usual diffraction limits. This capability has numerous fascinating applications!

One particularly impactful application is known as "Electric-Field Enhancement". Here, plasmonic resonances significantly amplify incoming electric fields at specific "hotspots" (think lowering reaction energy barriers, greatly enhanced molecular sensing, and precisely control electron kinetics)

Now imagine coupling two or more plasmonic materials, similar to coupled oscillators (the spring-mass systems from you favourite book on mechanics). As you bring these nanostructures closer together, fascinating phenomena like plasmonic charge transfer start to emerge. So, they can even be used as "switches" on command. My Masters work was precisely this, looking at plasmon-plasmon interactions in Transition-Metal Dichacogenide Nanoflakes. They have these localised edge plasmons (1-D like) that can be potentially used to look at nano-plasmonic "circuits".
